    Depth Comp

    A sketch of freediving mechanics: one breath, down a line to a plate and back. Everything you do costs oxygen; the deep part is easy, the shallow part on the way up is where it bites.

    Breathe-up

    • Space hold to inhale (a short press is a small breath, held longer it fills), let go to exhale. Slow and calm raises relaxed; fast deep breathing is hyperventilation: no urge to breathe later, and an earlier blackout.
    • P pack: gulps of air on top of a full breath. More oxygen, a later Frenzel limit, safer deep lungs; but more lift to kick against, dizziness, a stiff throat, and too much for too long faints you at the surface.
    • Enter dive (a duck dive), 1/2 fins or rope, [ ] target depth.

    Under water

    • Z X left and right leg (fins) or hand (rope). Alternate on an even beat: about one kick every half second, a pull every three quarters. Mashing or the same leg twice churns, twists you and burns oxygen.
    • ← → shift your weight: the head moves that way across the screen, head down or head up. Balance wanders by itself: keep the body straight down the line (watch Stream and Line). Slow, near neutral, head down is unstable.
    • ↑ arms ahead: the best streamline, the steadiest head first; ↓ arms by your sides: the relaxed freefall.
    • F Frenzel every metre or two as the ears press. It needs air in the mouth, which the lungs give until they are nearly empty (the Frenzel limit, ~20 m on a plain full breath). M mouthfill just before that: a mouthful locked in the cheeks carries the Frenzel on, about three times deeper in pressure. V Valsalva: fine head up near the surface, weak head down, and it blows the mouthfill.
    • T turn at the plate (or early). Kick up, then let the lift carry you the last metres.

    Surface

    • Space hook breaths, then O the OK sign, within 15 s. The brain's oxygen is lowest a few seconds after you surface.
    • R again, C camera distance (or wheel / pinch), H this help.
