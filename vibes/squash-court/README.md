# Squash Court

Finds a squash court in a photo, a video or the live camera from its painted lines, and recovers the camera that took it: position in metres, view direction, focal length and lens distortion. From that every pixel on the floor or walls maps to court coordinates, and the floor and front wall can be shown rectified. With the camera known, it also places the players and the ball in metres. Plain JS, no dependencies, runs in a worker.

Open `index.html` from any static http server (module worker), then Open… a photo or video, drop or paste one, use Camera, or press Demo frame for a synthetic frame with a known camera. Click the frame to measure a point; Export JSON writes K, R, C, the 3×4 matrix, the floor / front wall homographies, the players' positions, speeds, distance covered and heatmaps (seconds per 25 cm cell), and the ball's fitted flights with their velocities. A video plays with its sound (Sound on / off). Players & ball switches the player rings (x, y in metres), the ball's flights and their wall and floor impacts on the frame, the floor plan, the front wall and the 3D view, with each player's speed and the ball's speed in km/h; Heatmap shades the floor plan by where each player spent their time; Bounces marks every floor bounce on the floor plan and every front-wall hit on the rectified front wall, with a heat glow where they cluster, each dot in the colour of the player who hit the shot (grey: not known; a square: heard rather than seen). Player speed charts each player's speed over the last 30 s. The side panel (drag its edge to resize) shows the commentary feed, the floor plan, the front wall with every ball contact (the last one ringed in green, as is the last floor bounce), and each player's own heatmap with distance and top speed. Clear empties the measured points and everything gathered so far. Players: type the two names (kept per video file; ⇄ swaps them) and every label, heatmap title and commentary line uses them. Captions shows the newest commentary line over the video, subtitle-style, while the feed keeps the full log. A video has a time slider; seeking restarts the ball and player tracks from the new position. The floor plan and the front wall are drawn the way the main camera sees the court (facing the front wall, the right wall on the right). Speak reads the commentary aloud. Opening a `match.json` from `tools/match.mjs` shows a whole match: both players' heatmaps, their speed over the match, every bounce.

## How it works

The court is a known 3D object (WSF singles: 9.75 × 6.4 m, front out line 4.57, service line 1.78, tin 0.48, back wall line 2.13, short line 5.44 to its back edge, 1.6 m service boxes, 50 mm lines — `court.js`). The detector fits a full pinhole camera to it rather than a floor homography, because broadcast frames often show more wall than floor, and only a 3D model ties the two together.

1. **Line pixels** (`lineMap`): a pixel is on a painted line when the pixels a few px either side, across some direction, are alike and both differ from it the same way, at three scales. That fires on thin lines of any colour on wood, dark floors, white walls and glass, but not on edges. The four directional responses give the line normal; non-maximum suppression across it leaves sub-pixel centre lines.
2. **Segments** (`segments`): centre-line pixels are linked by direction and split until straight; short pieces (text, adverts, wood grain, crowd) are dropped.
3. **Distance map** of the segment pixels with the nearest one's index, so a projected model point finds its image line in O(1).
4. **Search** (`detectCourt`): ~6000 camera seeds drawn from where squash cameras stand (behind and above the back wall, overhead, through the side glass, in the court), scored by how much projected model line lands on image line; a short stochastic local search on the best; distinct survivors refined.
5. **Refinement** (`refine`): Gauss–Newton ICP on 8 parameters (Rodrigues rotation, position, log focal, radial k1), each model sample pulled along its projected normal to the nearest image line, Tukey-weighted with a shrinking scale, LM-damped, with plausibility bounds (above the floor, not behind the front wall). The back-wall line is ignored for cameras behind it.
6. **Service-box seeds** (`boxes.js`): the box sides, box backs and short line are found as fitted image lines; each pair of boxes gives the four inner corners, a floor homography, and (with the principal point known) a camera for a ladder of focal lengths. Corners come from intersecting lines, so a player's foot on a corner does not hide it.
7. **Choice**: every model line counts by the fraction of it that is matched (pixel length would reward a camera that blows up the few lines it matches and pushes the rest out of frame), the floor lines most, an image line pixel credits one model line only, and the image line must run the same way as the model line (glass panel seams and frames crossing it do not count). Candidates are also scored image-to-model, at 640 and 480 px work width. The focal / distance valley is probed for the best few, and the polish fits k1 and the tin height: 0.48 m (WSF) or 0.43 m (PSA men).
8. **Confidence**: a frame is a court when at least three lines match and one of them is on the floor; TV graphics, logos and audience shots match one or two lines of anything and are rejected.
9. **Video** (`track.js`): frames are tracked without the whole-frame line map. Each model line sample (every 10 cm) is projected with the last camera and a short colour profile is read across it straight from the frame at full resolution; the painted line is the peak of the same both-sides-alike response, nearest the prediction, in the colour learnt for that kind of line (so a glass seam or the scoreboard next to a faint red line loses), and it must continue along the model direction. That is a few thousand pixel reads instead of a pass over every pixel: 1–2 ms a frame, against ~50 ms for the full tracker.
   - **Held**: when the one Gauss–Newton step all the samples ask for together would move the court by under 0.2 px, the camera has not moved and is kept exactly as it is, so a fixed camera gives fixed coordinates (no new, slightly different fit every frame). The offsets seen on held frames are averaged and applied every 12 frames, so the fit gets finer the longer the camera holds.
   - **Pan / tilt / zoom**: Gauss–Newton on the profile peaks in windows of ±24, ±8 and ±3 px, with the camera position held (1 cm prior) on a **fixed mount**, the way broadcast and club cameras are used. That removes the zoom / distance valley a free fit slides along while zooming. The fit is also repeated with the position free; when that keeps fitting clearly better (cumulative evidence, not one frame), the mount is taken to move and the position follows the image with a constant-velocity prior.
   - **Fallbacks**: when it loses the court, or finds clearly fewer of its lines than usual, the full tracker (`trackCourt`, the line map at 480 px) runs and whichever camera finds more of the court is kept; the full tracker also takes the first frame after a detection when the video has played on for a while, and a moving camera seen at long intervals. Every camera held for a while is remembered: after a cut each is tried where it was (~1 ms), so returning to the main camera after a replay or a side camera needs no new detection. Otherwise the page re-detects after three lost frames. `?track=full` uses the full tracker every frame, `?mount=moving` drops the fixed-mount assumption.

### Players and ball (`players.js`, `ball.js`, `rally.js`)

10. **Players**: with the camera known, each pixel is a known point on the floor or a wall (glass walls the camera stands outside are looked through), so the court itself is the background: a robust quadratic colour model per region (floor, front wall, tin band), painted lines and seams masked, and what differs is foreground. People are found by occupancy over the floor: a 0.6 × 1.75 m person box is projected at every 10 cm of the court and scored by how much of it is foreground (an integral image), with feet on the floor and none below; the best is taken, its pixels explained away, and the next. The feet are the bottom outline of the foreground walked down from the body. Score bugs and captions (`overlays.js`, axis-aligned graphic boxes) are masked; when a player's feet are behind one (the person seen is too tall for feet where the visible part ends) they are placed from the head instead, assuming 1.72 m. Video keeps two identities with a constant-velocity tracker.
11. **Ball**: moving blobs in the middle of three distinct frames (repeated frames skipped: broadcast 25 fps in a 50 fps file), sized for a 40 mm ball at court depths with motion blur, outside the players' boxes and isolated (a blob on a swinging arm or a lunging leg sits in a field of motion). Candidates are linked into 2D tracks; before any 3D work a track must be smooth in time in the image (a quadratic in t fits it to 8 px), which drops most limbs and reflections cheaply; a finished track is then cut where one ballistic flight with air drag (24 g, 40 mm) stops fitting, each flight is fitted in 3D through the camera (start and velocity, Levenberg–Marquardt, the court's box of air as a soft bound), and impacts are labelled by a beam over {front, floor, side, back, racket}: after a wall the next flight starts at the impact point and time, after a racket it starts free near a player. Tracks that are still, slow (< 4 m/s: spectators through the glass), outside the court or badly fitted are dropped, and of flights overlapping in time (the ball and its reflection) the better supported is kept.
12. **Speeds and heatmap**: a player's speed is the least-squares slope of their tracked position over the last 0.6 s (detections come about 5 times a second and the feet jitter by centimetres), with distance covered and the top speed kept; the heatmap adds the time between detections to the player's 25 cm floor cell, blurred and scaled to each player's busiest cell. The ball's speed is the fitted flight's velocity at the current frame, so it slows along a flight with drag and gravity as the real ball does.
13. **Bounces** (`bounces.js`): the floor and front-wall impacts of every fitted flight, plus the end of a flight that comes down onto the floor (tracks are not joined across an impact, so most floor bounces end one), kept for the whole clip and binned in 25 cm cells.
14. **Sound** (`audio.js`): the soundtrack is decoded (the page decodes the opened file; the match tool uses ffmpeg) and impacts are sharp onsets above 2 kHz: energy in 2 ms frames, at least 15 dB over the median of the previous 150 ms, most of the rise within 6 ms. A heard impact with no fitted impact near it, where a flight that ended just before (carried forward with gravity and drag) or starts just after (carried back) reaches a surface within 30 cm, becomes a bounce found by ear; near a player at racket height it is a racket hit. The audio delay is estimated from impacts seen in the picture, and only used when it matches clearly more impacts than chance (a few onsets a second match some impacts at any delay).
15. **Who hit it**: the last flight before an impact that heads for the front wall left a racket; it is carried back to the racket sound just before it (or 40 ms) and the nearer player there hit the shot, if clearly nearer than the other.
16. **A whole match** (`tools/match.mjs <video> <out>`): the file is cut into chunks run in parallel processes, each decoding raw frames from ffmpeg, tracking the court through cuts (sparse tracking, remembered cameras, a fresh detection at most twice a second), running players and ball on every stretch where the court is seen and finding impact sounds. Identities across cuts come from shirt colour: each player track of one camera stretch gets its mean chest colour (the person's own foreground pixels), those split into two groups by hue, with the two tracks of one stretch kept apart. Then per player the heatmap, distance, speed over time, and every bounce with its hitter. Fixed things the colour model keeps calling foreground (door brackets, the bars under a score bug) are learnt per camera stretch and masked. Writes `match.json` for the page and a `match.png` floor plan.
17. **Commentary** (`commentary.js`): every shot is built around its front-wall contact, measured or inferred (a flight heading forward carried on to the wall, or one coming back carried back to it), from the high main camera only. Its pace (fastest seen on the way in), whether a side wall came first (boast), the front-wall height, the apex and first bounce after, and the closest pass to a side wall on the way back name it: lob (above 2.6 m, apex over 3.6 m, under 32 m/s), drop (under 0.95 m and slow, landing in front), kill (low and fast), boast or drive; straight or crosscourt (struck on one side, ending clearly on the other), length (short, length, deep), tight (within 35 cm of a side wall), pace (hard from 40 m/s; a speed outside what the shot allows is left out). Movement lines when a player sprints into, or is pinned in, a corner. A refit rewrites its shot's line in place; shots with little behind them are faded.
18. **In the page**: the players and the ball run in two more workers fed with frames the court worker has fitted, so neither slows the camera tracking. The ball worker is fed every new video frame (`requestVideoFrameCallback`, 640 px, the latest camera), queued up to 12 deep, since the three-frame difference needs consecutive frames; the court worker only analyses some of them.

## Numbers

- PSA broadcast video (two top-10-rally compilations, 1280×720, one frame every 10 s, 169 frames, not in the repo; `node tools/batch.mjs out frames/*.jpg`): 138 detected with confidence. Every wide broadcast view checked by eye fits, including the PSA men's tin; court-level close-ups from inside the glass mostly fit; the end cards, logos and audience shots are rejected. Misses: older dark courts with faint blue lines (Hull, El Gouna), some steep overhead zooms and corner close-ups.

- Synthetic frames (`node tools/synth_eval.mjs 20`, random broadcast cameras, club and glass courts with players and advert clutter): 17/20 found; on those median error 1.6 cm in position, 0.03° in view direction, 0.08 % in focal, 0.18 px reprojection.
- 18 PSA / SquashTV broadcast stills (YouTube auto-thumbnails of match videos, not in the repo): 11 fitted correctly by eye — every wide or standard broadcast view, overhead club cameras and glass show courts. The misses are close-ups where little of the court shows (one wall and a corner of floor), which have no unique solution from a single frame; in a video they are carried by tracking.
- Time (node, one core): detection ≈ 0.9–1.5 s per still (both resolutions, both tin heights). Tracking (`node tools/track_eval.mjs <clip>`), per 960 px frame: sparse 1–2 ms when held, 3–7 ms when panning or zooming, against ≈ 50 ms for the full tracker.
- Tracking accuracy on synthetic clips with the true camera of every frame (960 px, 25 fps): a fixed-mount pan, hold and 40 % zoom (`tools/make_ptz_video.mjs`, 150 frames): camera position within 1.6 cm on every frame and floor points within 0.4 cm (95th percentile), the full tracker drifting to 3.9 m along the zoom valley; a camera that dollies 1.5 m while zooming (`tools/make_video.mjs`): within 10 cm, floor 3.3 cm (95th percentile), full tracker 2.4 m / 50 cm.
- Tracking real PSA broadcast footage (1280×720 compilations, 6–30 s clips at 25 fps): the main cameras are locked off, and 90–99 % of frames are held, with the court not moving by a single pixel between frames (the full tracker moves it 0.4–0.9 px a frame). The full-resolution fit lands visibly closer on the painted lines than the 480 px full tracker.

- Players on synthetic frames with the true camera (`TRUTH=1 node tools/players_eval.mjs 20`): 10 of 15 visible players, median 1.7 cm, 90th percentile 5.7 cm (the misses are players half out of frame or behind the glass seams); with the detected camera ≈ 3 cm. On PSA broadcast frames the feet land where they should on wide views, including the reverse angle; on overhead zooms they can sit a few tens of cm fore or aft of the contact. ≈ 150–300 ms per frame at 480–640 px.
- Ball on synthetic rallies (`node tools/ball_eval.mjs 20`, a drive off the front wall and floor, 1 px noise): median 2.4 cm, 90th percentile 9 cm per frame in 3D with the true cuts; cut automatically (`AUTO=1`) 2.6 / 14 cm.
- Whole files (`node tools/match.mjs a.mp4 out --jobs 30`): a 15-minute PSA compilation in about 3 minutes on 30 processes, 13.4 minutes of it with the court in view; player heat centres on the T, floor bounces cluster in the back corners and the service-box areas. The audio delay came out at 50 ms on one file (27 impacts matched against 11 by chance) and was not determined on the other. A compilation is many matches, so "two players" by shirt colour only means something within one match; the hitter is known for about a third of the bounces.
- Commentary on the two PSA files: 164 and 142 shots named from the main camera (drives about 80 %, then crosscourts, drops, kills, lobs, boasts — roughly the mix of professional squash); on synthetic shots (straight and crosscourt drive, lob, drop, boast, an inferred contact) every one is named right. Single real shots are not yet checked against the picture one by one.
- Ball on PSA footage (`node tools/rally_run.mjs a.mp4 out 140 4`, 4 s clips): the ball is found on wide views when it is in front of the glass or the floor and not inside a player's box, and its fitted flights sit on it in the frame, but many flights are short or labelled loosely, and some 3D starts are wrong in depth (a flight off the racket has no plane to pin it). ≈ 10–20 ms per frame for the ball.

## Limits

Assumes a standard singles court and a principal point at the image centre. Painted lines must be visible; a frame showing one wall and a sliver of floor is ambiguous. On show courts the side-wall out line rarely matches the WSF slope exactly, so it weighs least. A camera that really moves (dolly, hand-held) is only recognised after a few frames, during which the fixed-mount prior pulls it back. A cut to a camera not seen before relies on a new detection, which can pick a wrong fit on blue-line courts. The full tracker still runs on doubtful frames, and those cost ≈ 50 ms.

Players: two at most, found per frame; a player hidden by the other, or crouched behind a score bug (placed as if standing), is misplaced, and a player half out of frame is not found. Speeds inherit the position errors: a player placed from the head behind a graphic, or a coasting track, gives a wrong speed for those moments, and the heatmap counts it. Sound: onsets include shoe squeaks, the crowd and the commentary; most heard impacts are never used, but a wrong one near the end of a flight can add a wrong bounce. Slow-motion replays shot from the main camera are analysed like live play and give wrong ball speeds and repeated bounces. Ball: a 40 mm ball is 2–5 px on a 720p broadcast, so it is lost against the players, the tin, and the lines; at 25 fps it moves up to 2 m between frames on a drive, so a racket-to-racket flight has little to fix its depth; the camera is assumed fixed during a flight.
